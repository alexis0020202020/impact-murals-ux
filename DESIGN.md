# Impact Murals — Design Direction

## Central visual idea
The website is built around an **Impact Field**: a controlled system of pressure, displacement, fracture lines, layered surfaces, and return to alignment.

This is not a literal explosion aesthetic. It is a spatial design language derived from the logo and from the physical nature of murals and public art.

The site should feel connected to:
- surfaces;
- façades;
- scale;
- material;
- site integration;
- layers;
- the passage from direction to execution.

Combine:
- the authority and scale of an architecture studio;
- the cultural quality of a contemporary art studio;
- the clarity and conversion logic of a high-performing B2B landing page.

## Desired feeling
- Monumental
- Editorial
- Architectural
- Contemporary
- Tactile
- Confident
- Visually impressive
- Restrained but not boring

## Ownable visual system
The design must use one coherent grammar rather than a collection of effects.

### The Impact Field
- Establish one invisible impact point or axis in the hero.
- Type, rules, image masks, and metadata may shift along vectors radiating from that point.
- Fracture lines from the logo may become grid interruptions, crop edges, or reveal paths.
- Elements settle into precise alignment rather than remaining chaotic.
- The same logic should reappear in project reveals and transitions, with restraint.

### Surface plates
Project media should feel like architectural surfaces or material planes, not generic cards.
- Images may expand, overlap, crop, or align against fracture lines.
- Metadata sits on the grid like project notation.
- Different media roles use different proportions.
- Do not wrap every image inside a rounded container.

### Scale notation
Use restrained project notation as part of the visual identity:
`PUBLIC REALM · DUBAI, UAE · SCALE TO BE CONFIRMED · CREATIVE DIRECTION + PRODUCTION`

Never invent facts. Placeholder values must be visibly labelled.

## Hero
The hero must not depend on one dominant project photograph.

Use:
- oversized typography;
- strong spatial composition;
- a precise but interrupted architectural grid;
- controlled displacement derived from the Impact Field;
- subtle depth;
- the animated Impact Murals logo;
- a clear proposition and visible CTA;
- a strong transition into the first visual proof.

Working hero content:

Eyebrow:
`ART DIRECTION · PUBLIC ART · LARGE-SCALE PRODUCTION`

Headline:
`WE SHAPE AND DELIVER ART FOR BRANDS, SPACES AND THE PUBLIC REALM.`

Support:
`From early creative direction and artist curation to site-specific development and large-scale production.`

CTA:
`DISCUSS A PROJECT`

Secondary:
`VIEW SELECTED WORK`

The copy is provisional and editable. The layout must tolerate reasonable copy changes.

## Signature interactions
The V1 should contain a small number of bespoke signatures, not dozens of effects.

### 1. Logo animation
Use `public/assets/logo/impact-murals.svg`.
Animate mainly `impact-fragment-*` using the embedded motion-vector data.
Keep `murals-wordmark` mostly stable.
Create a short, premium, controlled-impact effect with subtle depth.
No loop, no splash screen, no cartoon explosion.

### 2. Hero-to-work transition
The typographic hero should open along the Impact Field and reveal the first project surface.
The transition should feel like pressure creating an opening into the physical work.
It must not be a generic full-screen image zoom copied from a UI demo.

### 3. Project presentation system
Avoid a conventional gallery as the main experience.
Use changing media roles:
- environment panorama;
- architectural view;
- portrait detail;
- production image;
- material detail;
- diptych or sequence.

The project system should communicate context, artwork, detail, production, and real-world scale.

## Page rhythm
The page should feel like one continuous narrative, not a stack of obvious blocks.

Psychological sequence:
1. Positioning and action
2. Curiosity and first visual reveal
3. Proof through three project contexts
4. Understanding of Impact Murals' end-to-end role
5. Deeper work proof
6. Operational reassurance
7. Studio and social proof
8. Action

## Main content movements
### Movement 1 — Position
Typographic hero, logo animation, immediate CTA.

### Movement 2 — Reveal and demonstrate
Transition into project imagery and reveal the three main project contexts through actual visual proof rather than service cards.

### Movement 3 — Explain the partnership
Communicate one creative partner from first direction to final delivery, including the reality of production.

### Movement 4 — Reassure and convert
Selected projects, operational credibility, studio, logos, and final CTA.

## Three contexts
These should feel like immersive chapters or visual sequences, not small cards.

### Public Art & Large-Scale Murals
Visible interventions for façades, developments, public spaces, destinations, and urban surfaces.

### Brand & Retail Environments
Physical expressions of brand identity that people can enter, experience, photograph, and remember.

### Hospitality, Property & Destinations
Art that creates a distinctive sense of place across hotels, communities, developments, and shared environments.

## End-to-end role
Working direction:

`ONE CREATIVE PARTNER, FROM FIRST DIRECTION TO FINAL DELIVERY.`

Narrative:
- Clarify the role art should play.
- Shape the concept around the site and audience.
- Bring in the right artistic language and talent.
- Develop the response for real-world production.
- Coordinate execution through final delivery.

Do not render this as five icon cards.
The visual treatment should feel connected to layers becoming aligned and executable.

## Operational reassurance
Working line:

`CREATIVE AMBITION, GROUNDED IN REAL-WORLD DELIVERY.`

Integrate:
- indoor and outdoor production;
- large surfaces;
- access and site planning;
- material selection;
- artist coordination;
- scheduling;
- final handover.

Use layout, typography, detail imagery, and metadata instead of a generic capability grid.

## Studio
Present Impact Murals as a focused art direction and production studio supported by a curated network of artists and specialists selected according to each project.

The founder's long execution background is evidence that the creative direction is grounded in reality. Do not frame the company as only a personal graffiti practice. Do not pretend there is a large permanent corporate team.

## Social proof
Use monochrome client/collaborator logos near the end with generous spacing and restrained movement.

Safe working heading:
`SELECTED BRANDS, DESTINATIONS AND COLLABORATORS FEATURED ACROSS OUR WORK.`

## Final CTA
Working content:

`HAVE A LOCATION, A BRIEF OR SIMPLY AN EARLY IDEA?`

`Share what you are planning. Impact Murals can help define the right artistic direction and determine what the project requires.`

CTA:
`DISCUSS A PROJECT`

## Color and typography
Do not lock a random trendy palette before the identity spine is tested.
Start from:
- warm charcoal or ink;
- off-white or mineral white;
- neutral stone tones;
- one optional signal accent used sparingly.

Use a strong display face plus a precise neutral sans-serif. Avoid the default AI pairing of an overly fashionable serif with a generic grotesk unless the choice is clearly justified.

## Avoid
- centered generic hero composition;
- three service cards below the hero;
- excessive pills;
- generic icon grids;
- random gradients;
- glassmorphism;
- stock illustration;
- portfolio masonry as the primary work experience;
- identical section layouts;
- repetitive fade-up animation;
- over-designed cursor gimmicks;
- component-library visual inconsistency;
- motion without purpose.

## Mobile
Mobile must be intentionally designed.
- Maintain typographic impact without broken wrapping.
- Simplify or remove nonessential parallax and depth.
- Keep CTA access immediate.
- Preserve project context and metadata readability.
- Ensure the logo animation remains light and stable.
- Recompose the Impact Field rather than merely shrinking desktop.
