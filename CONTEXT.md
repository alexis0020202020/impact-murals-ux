# Impact Murals: business and website context

## Positioning

Impact Murals is an artist-led art direction and production studio serving projects in Dubai and the UAE. It helps clients use art for communication, identity, experience and the transformation of spaces.

Murals are a central expertise and an acquisition entry point, not the limit of the studio. The studio is neither a generic full-service agency nor simply a supplier selling wall painting by square metre.

Its contribution can include understanding a brief, defining art's role, creative development, artist selection, adapting a concept to a site or format, coordinating artists and delivering the work.

It can also join an existing team or execute an approved artistic direction. Do not imply that every client must buy a complete strategy before commissioning an artwork.

## Commercial goal of the site

A prospect may arrive from a cold email, Instagram, a recommendation or a search result. They need to understand quickly:

- Is this relevant to my project?
- Can these people understand my intentions and constraints?
- Can they create the work, not only talk about it?
- Can they work in the style or role the project needs?
- What is the easiest way to start a conversation?

The homepage should orient and establish credibility. Offer pages should answer a specific need even when visited directly. Articles and guides should use the same visual system and lead naturally towards a relevant offer or enquiry.

The goal is an operational, distinctive and convincing site, not an endless redesign or a content encyclopaedia on the homepage.

## Three commercial entry points

### Art for Brands

Art as part of brand communication and experience.

Clients may be brand/marketing teams, creative directors, event or experiential agencies, founders or direct commissioners.

Possible formats include launches, activations, events, live painting, collaborations with artists, branded murals, artwork applied to packaging and other communication materials. An artwork can become the subject of photography, video and campaign content.

Do not reduce this to putting a logo on a mural. Do not promise a full advertising agency, media-buying service or complete audiovisual production by default. Where content capture or additional specialists are relevant, frame them as an agreed project scope rather than an automatic deliverable.

### Art for Places

Art developed for a physical environment, in dialogue with its design intent.

Clients may be interior designers, architects, hospitality teams, developers, restaurants, workplaces and direct commissioners.

The studio should understand the existing palette, materials, atmosphere, architecture, audience and use. It can propose commissioned canvases, bespoke artwork, murals, site-specific interventions and an appropriate artist or visual language.

Reassure designers that their concept will be respected and enriched. Do not imply that Impact Murals imposes one signature style or replaces the interior designer.

### Public Art & Large-Scale Murals

Artistic interventions whose site and execution require specialist attention.

Clients may be agencies, developers, destinations, venues, institutions, organisations, property owners or public-sector commissioners. It is not restricted to government tenders or enormous walls.

The need may be concept development, adapting approved artwork, working on an exterior facade, planning access, selecting materials, sequencing work or coordinating artists.

Communicate creative competence and execution capability without turning the page into a procurement manual. Responsibilities depend on the agreed scope; do not claim permits, engineering or specialist services not established by the project.

### Across all three

Artist selection and coordination are shared strengths, not a fourth offer. A curated network can support different visual languages. Avoid the absolute claim that the studio can deliver literally every style.

Live painting and workshops are formats within suitable projects, not competing top-level offers.

A project may overlap pillars. Let visitors ask for guidance rather than requiring perfect self-classification.

## Proof, not mandatory case studies

The user does not want the site organised around traditional case studies or a portfolio that makes clients infer the offer.

No obligatory Challenge / Strategy / Solution / Results structure.

Visual work can still establish credibility: imagery, a short context, an accurate role, location when known and useful details. A Work page is compatible with this direction; it must not replace the three-offer navigation.

Do not invent client names, projects, performance numbers, testimonials or collaborator logos. Do not portray a curated network as a large permanent staff.

Provisional images are acceptable for designing the layout. They are not evidence of completed work. Do not remove their current exclusion rules or create fictitious project URLs to fill the site.

## Voice

Confident, artist-led, clear, contemporary and commercially useful. Premium through discernment, precision and care, not inflated luxury language.

Public copy: English. Discussion and handoff to the user: French.

Avoid:

- graffiti as the defining commercial label;
- “we bring walls to life” and empty creative slogans;
- a price-per-square-metre identity;
- overpromising a complete branding agency;
- long defensive disclaimers;
- em dashes;
- endless uppercase slogans without practical explanation.

Keep room for early ideas, smaller commissions and clients bringing a defined scope. Do not create eligibility barriers with compulsory budgets or dimensions.

## Visual ambition

Keep the fragmented animated Impact Murals logo. It is part of the identity, not an intro to remove. Its size and timing must coexist with an immediately understandable proposition and CTA, especially on mobile.

Target: rigour underneath, freedom on the surface. Architectural confidence, contemporary artistic energy and clear B2B orientation.

Light, breathable surfaces can coexist with deliberate dark moments and a restrained energetic accent. Art should ultimately carry much of the colour.

Expressive composition does not mean unclear navigation. Use controlled asymmetry, scale, image proportions and a few distinctive motions rather than a collection of effects.

## What exists already

The corrected V2-80 already has:

- a light/off-white canvas with dark interruptions;
- the three commercial offer pages;
- editorial sections rather than generic service cards;
- a fragmented logo and secondary fragment motion;
- automatic article and guide presentation;
- a separate enquiry route;
- technical exclusion of placeholder project pages and related links.

These are starting assets, not claims that the UX is finished. Reassess them in the browser before changing them. Previous comments such as “the site is dark-heavy” are not automatically true of this version.
