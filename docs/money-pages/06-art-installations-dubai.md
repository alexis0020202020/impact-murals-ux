### 2 · Site opportunities and formats

**Purpose:** show what a site can take, without a catalogue of every material.

**Headline:** WHAT THE SITE CAN TAKE.

**Copy**

An installation starts with a real place. The form is decided after the site and the brief are understood. It might be a painted or mixed-media wall feature, work developed across panels, a dimensional piece, or a suspended or freestanding element, within what the artist and the specialists can deliver.

- A NICHE OR A VOID. An integrated piece made for the opening.
- AN ATRIUM OR DOUBLE HEIGHT. A suspended or tall freestanding form.
- A CIRCULATION ROUTE. Work that develops as you walk it.
- COLUMNS AND ARCHITECTURAL ELEMENTS. Work that wraps or integrates.
- A TEMPORARY SPACE. An installation for an opening, a campaign or a festival.

**Composition:** a ruled list of five site types in display type; each line carries a small monospace dimension note (for example: H 6 M / W 3 M, ILLUSTRATIVE) beside a thin-line sketch. No photographs of unrelated spaces.

**Media:** ESSENTIAL: ILLUSTRATIVE site sketches. OPTIONAL: real site photographs. TO SOURCE.

### 4 · How the idea becomes buildable (centrepiece)

**Purpose:** be the page's argument: the joints are managed.

**Headline:** FROM IDEA TO SOMETHING THAT STANDS.

**Copy**

The idea is the easy part to approve. The joints are where an installation is won or lost: a weight no one checked, a fixing no one owns, an access route nobody walked. We work through those questions with the artist before anything is made.

01 SITE. Dimensions, structure readiness, light and key views.
02 MATERIAL. What it is made of, its weight, finish and exposure.
03 FIXING. How it attaches, and who is responsible for the fixing.
04 SAMPLE OR PROTOTYPE. A physical test before the commitment.
05 MAINTENANCE AND LIFETIME. Cleaning, access for care, and how long it is meant to last.
06 INSTALLATION. Transport, lifting, the access route and the window.

**Composition:** the widest and tallest section of the page. Six drawn frames across the full width (an ILLUSTRATIVE development diagram that follows one abstract form from site survey to installed), each with a dimension line and a numeral, the text beneath. On tablet, three by two; on mobile, a vertical drawn sequence.

**Media:** ESSENTIAL: the development diagram, labelled "Illustrative development". Replace panel by panel with real process photographs when they exist. OPTIONAL: one real sample or prototype photograph beside item 04. TO SOURCE.

### 5 · Artists and specialist responsibilities

**Purpose:** make the responsibility map explicit, with no implied engineering sign-off by Impact.

**Headline:** WHO IS RESPONSIBLE FOR WHAT.

**Copy**

- IMPACT MURALS. Artistic direction, artist selection, coordination of the specialists, production planning and the artistic review.
- THE ARTIST. Authorship and concept, and the hands-on making of their own elements.
- FABRICATOR. Structural and dimensional elements made to drawings, as the work requires.
- ENGINEER. Structural sign-off on loads and fixings, where the work needs it.
- LIGHTING AND ELECTRICAL. Where the work is lit or powered.
- INSTALLER. Transport, lifting, rigging and fixing.
- SITE OWNER OR CONTRACTOR. Readiness of the structure, access and building approvals.

Where a piece needs engineering sign-off, a qualified engineer appointed for the project provides it. Impact Murals does not certify structures. Each proposal names who does what. Not every project needs every role.

**Composition:** a full-width ledger, seven rows, hairline rules, the function in display type on the left and the responsibility on the right. The Impact row carries a thin accent rule. It is a real HTML table with row headers.

**Media:** none. This module is type, deliberately.

### 11 · Questions, guides and the site brief

**Purpose:** resolve responsibility, feasibility, lifetime and scope questions.

**Headline (FAQ):** WHAT TO SETTLE FIRST.

**FAQ**

1. **Is this commissioning, or installing art you already own?** Commissioning: a new work made for the site. Hanging or placing existing artworks is a different service.
2. **Can it be temporary or permanent?** Both. The choice shapes materials, fixings, care and who is responsible for each.
3. **Can we bring our own artist?** Yes. We can work with an artist you already have, or select one by the brief, the site and the form the work will take. Either way we coordinate the specialists the idea needs.
4. **Can you produce an installation from an approved concept?** Yes. We assess buildability, define the production route and coordinate the specialists. We tell you what may need to change to make the concept stand.
5. **Which parts do you make yourselves?** It depends on the work. The artist makes what the artist should make. Structural and dimensional elements are produced by fabricators suited to the material, coordinated by us. The proposal names who produces what before you appoint.
6. **Who is responsible for engineering and fixings?** Qualified specialists appointed for the project, as the work requires. We separate the artistic feasibility review from technical certification, in writing.
7. **Will we see a sample or prototype?** Where the form, the material or the risk calls for it, a sample or prototype is scoped and approved before production.
8. **How are access, transport and installation handled?** They are reviewed during feasibility and scoped as their own lines. The structure and the access route must be ready before installation starts.
9. **What is handed over, and how long does it take?** A record of the work and care notes, as defined in the proposal. The timeline depends on the concept, the materials, the approvals and the site, and we give a programme once the site is known.

**Headline (guides):** BUILDABILITY GUIDES.

**Five guides** (see section E).

**Final brief, headline:** WHAT HAS TO STAND, AND WHERE?

**Final brief, copy:** The location, the type of space, the objective, whether it is temporary or permanent, a target date and rough dimensions. Plans and photographs help. Technical details can wait until we have scoped the work.

**Composition:** shared closing pattern. The form hides all technical fields behind an optional "add technical details" expander.

**Claims fence for this page.** The copy is written so it stays true with no spatial work on file: it claims *artistic direction and coordination*, never in-house fabrication, engineering sign-off, or any specific material, suspended or outdoor scope. Zero case studies is acceptable. Zero evidence of the actual proposed medium is not a reason to inflate capability. Do not add a material or a scale claim until a documented example supports it. The best external corroboration is an artist, fabricator or design-team reference that states which party directed, fabricated, engineered and installed.
