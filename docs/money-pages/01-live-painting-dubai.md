### 2 · Audience experience

**Purpose:** show what guests see and what the event keeps, and make clear that visibility is a programme decision.

**Headline:** WHAT YOUR GUESTS SEE.

**Copy**

A finished artwork gives an event one moment. Live painting gives it a progression. Guests see the first marks, come back as the image builds and are in the room when it resolves.

The performance can be quiet and continuous, or built to land on a single cue. How visible the artist is, from the edge of the room to centre stage, is something we decide with you.

Frame captions:

- FIRST MARKS. A blank surface gives guests a reason to stop.
- THE BUILD. The work is different each time they pass.
- THE REVEAL. The finished piece, in the room, with the brand around it.

**Composition:** a three-frame horizontal sequence at one height, captions in monospace beneath each frame, generous space around the paragraph. On mobile the frames stack with the caption above each.

**Media**

- ESSENTIAL: three frames of one real piece at three stages, with the audience visible in at least the middle frame. EXISTS (partial): frames cut from `BMW_BINANCE_MAIN` and `BMW_BINANCE_DETAIL`. TO SOURCE: a middle frame showing guests watching.

### 3 · Format choices

**Purpose:** let the buyer pick a format, with timing as a variable and the output stated.

**Headline:** FOUR WAYS TO RUN IT.

**Copy** (a ruled list in display type, each line a name and its text)

- CONTINUOUS CREATION. The artist works across the event while guests move around them. It suits receptions, exhibitions and longer programmes. You end with a finished artwork by close.
- TIMED REVEAL. The work is planned backwards from a cue: a speech, an unveiling, a countdown. It suits launches and keynotes. You end with the piece revealed on the cue, where the programme and technique allow it.
- LIVE MURAL OR PANEL. A larger surface, painted by one artist or a small team. It can stay as a backdrop afterwards or be installed later. You end with a work that outlasts the evening.
- LIVE ON A VEHICLE. One hero object painted as the performance. We have painted vehicles live at launches and events, including in Dubai. You end with the painted vehicle. *(Link: Automotive.)*

Closing line: Duration depends on the artist, the technique and the scale. The proposal gives an estimate, never a promise of how long a painting will take.

**Composition:** four ruled rows at full width, the name in display type on the left, the text on the right, one small illustrative frame per row at the far right (optional). No cards, no icons.

**Media:** OPTIONAL, one small frame per row. EXISTS for the vehicle row (`BMW_BINANCE_DETAIL`). TO SOURCE for the other three. Do not use a stock event image to fill a row.

**Specialist formats:** digital or screen-based live formats are **not** listed. They stay out until a specific artist and format are confirmed.

### 6 · Performance production

**Purpose:** answer the producer's practical questions before they ask.

**Headline:** THE PRACTICAL PART, SETTLED BEFORE THE DAY.

**Copy**

Live work has a fixed window and no second take, so these questions are answered in advance.

- SURFACE AND SCALE. How large the work is, what it is painted on, and how it is displayed or removed.
- SIGHTLINES. Where guests stand, and where the cameras do.
- LIGHT. Enough to paint by, and to film by.
- PROTECTION. Floors, walls and stand finishes covered.
- MATERIALS AND AIR. Chosen for the venue. Indoor events agree ventilation and odour with the venue.
- LOAD-IN AND STRIKE. Access, timing and what leaves the venue afterwards.
- HEAT AND WIND. For outdoor events, shade, wind and surface temperature shape the plan.

Caption on the diagram: Example layout only. Every setup is drawn for its venue.

**Composition:** left, a plan-view diagram (stage footprint, artist zone, guest zone, camera line, load-in route) drawn in the site's thin-line language; right, the ruled list. Beside or below, a real making image so the diagram is not the only evidence.

**Media**

- ESSENTIAL: ILLUSTRATIVE setup diagram, labelled "Illustrative layout".
- ESSENTIAL: one wider setup view showing scale with people and surface in frame. TO SOURCE.
- OPTIONAL: a protection and preparation detail.

### 7 · Run of show and content

**Purpose:** give the event team the timeline, the responsibility split and the fate of the artwork.

**Headline:** FROM FIRST CUE TO HANDOVER.

**Copy**

An event team needs to know who does what, and when. The shape is usually the same.

BEFORE DOORS: set-up and protection. START CUE: the artist begins. THE BUILD: the work develops, with agreed checkpoints if the format is timed. REVEAL CUE: only if the format has one. HANDOVER: strike, and the finished work handed over.

WHO DOES WHAT

- ARTIST AND IMPACT MURALS. The artwork, the painting set-up and the performance.
- YOU OR YOUR AGENCY. The programme, the cues, the venue and access.
- YOUR AV AND CONTENT TEAM. Cameras, screens and editing, if commissioned.

We do not assume camera, screen or editing support. If you want them, we plan the sightlines and key moments with your crew and the proposal states what is included.

THE FINISHED PIECE. Ownership, storage, installation and permitted use of images are agreed in the proposal, before the event. The work can stay with the brand, be installed later or simply complete the evening.

**Composition:** a horizontal timeline across the full width with five markers, the three responsibility columns below it. Mobile: the timeline turns vertical and the columns become a stacked list.

**Media:** OPTIONAL: a photograph of the finished work in place after the event. TO SOURCE.

### 10 · Fee factors

**Purpose:** explain what drives a live painting fee without publishing a bargain rate.

**Headline:** WHAT SHAPES THE FEE.

**Copy**

Two groups of variables, kept apart on purpose.

- THE ARTISTRY. The artist and technique. New direction or adaptation of approved artwork. The surface and scale. How long the performance runs. Any rehearsal.
- THE PRODUCTION. Team size. Materials and protection. Venue access, travel and load-in. Content support. Rights and use of the finished work.

We do not publish an hourly rate. Performance length is only one variable among several. Send the date, venue and window and we will tell you what a proposal would cover.

**Composition:** two columns, type only, with a quiet hairline between them.

### 11 · Questions, guides and the brief

**Purpose:** remove booking objections, route to six planning guides, and close.

**Headline (FAQ):** WHAT PRODUCERS ASK.

**FAQ**

1. **How far ahead should we book?** As early as the date is known. Artist availability and venue approvals set the timeline more than the painting does. If your date is close, send it anyway and we will say plainly whether it is feasible.
2. **Can the artwork be finished at a fixed moment?** Often, when the format is planned around it. A timed reveal is built backwards from the cue. Treat any duration we quote as a programme estimate, confirmed with the artist in the proposal, not a guaranteed finish time.
3. **Can we choose the artist, or do you?** Either. We select the artist whose technique and visual language suit the event, or build the format around an artist you already have in mind. When the audience is watching, presence and pace matter as much as style.
4. **Can you paint artwork we have already approved?** Yes. We adapt approved campaign artwork for live execution and tell you what changes when a design meets a live surface and a fixed window. You are never asked to review speculative artwork before you appoint us.
5. **Can it run indoors and outdoors?** Both are possible. Indoors, material choice and ventilation are agreed with the venue. Outdoors, sun, wind and surface temperature shape the plan. We review the space before we propose.
6. **What happens to the finished artwork?** It is decided before the event. The work can stay with the brand, be installed later or complete the evening as a one-off. The proposal records ownership and how the work and its images may be used.
7. **Can it be filmed or shown on screens?** Yes, if it is planned. We set sightlines and key moments with your content or AV team. Cameras, screens and editing are not included unless the proposal says so.
8. **Is this for weddings or private parties?** Our live work is built for brands and organisations. If your event is private, send the brief and we will tell you whether it fits.

**Headline (guides):** PLANNING A LIVE EVENT.

**Final brief, headline:** THE DATE, THE VENUE, THE CROWD.

**Final brief, copy:** Event type, date, venue and audience are enough. If the date is provisional or the direction is open, say so. We will work with what you have.
