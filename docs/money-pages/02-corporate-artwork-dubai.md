### 2 · The brief and the brand

**Purpose:** establish that brand guidelines are an input and that the artistic answer is developed, not pasted.

**Headline:** FROM BRAND GUIDELINES TO AN ARTISTIC ANSWER.

**Copy**

Brand guidelines tell us who you are. They do not tell us what a wall should say. We read them for colour, tone and values, then look at the architecture, the audience and what the workplace is for. The artist's own language does the rest.

A logo on a wall is rarely good art. Where a mark or a line of text belongs in the work, we place it on purpose, not by default.

**Composition:** editorial two columns. Left: one large close detail of a painted surface, cropped to texture. Right: the two short paragraphs with generous top whitespace. No moodboard graphic.

**Media**

- ESSENTIAL: one large close-up of original brushwork or airbrush detail. EXISTS (`FERRARI_REALISM` detail crop, `PORSCHE_PORTRAIT`).
- OPTIONAL: a real brand-input and finished-art pairing, only with the client's permission. Never a fabricated brand moodboard.

### 3 · Workplace applications

**Purpose:** show where art goes in a workplace and what is decided in each place.

**Headline:** ZONE BY ZONE.

**Copy** (a ruled list; each line is a zone and its commissioning decision)

- ARRIVAL AND RECEPTION. The first work every visitor meets. One strong piece, sized for how people wait and for the light in the room.
- BOARDROOMS AND MEETING ROOMS. Presence without distraction. Quiet palettes, careful scale, work that holds up behind a speaker on a video call.
- CORRIDORS AND SHARED SPACE. Long, narrow and walked past. Sequence matters: a series reads differently from a single piece.
- FLOORS, ATRIA AND STAIRWELLS. A multi-floor programme with one thread and different works, delivered in phases.

Closing line: One room is a perfectly good place to start.

**Composition:** a wide, thin-line plan strip across the top (an abstract floor plate with four numbered positions), the four ruled lines below it on the same grid. All text is visible without hover. Mobile: the list first, the plan strip below.

**Media:** ESSENTIAL: the plan strip, ILLUSTRATIVE, labelled "Illustrative plan". OPTIONAL: one real photograph per zone as they are sourced.

### 4 · Choosing the art format

**Purpose:** let a buyer pick a format and be routed to its specialist page.

**Headline:** MURAL, CANVAS, SERIES OR SOMETHING DIMENSIONAL.

**Copy** (four ruled rows; each has three short fields)

- MURAL. Painted on the wall, on site. Suits reception walls and feature walls. Worth knowing: it needs a prepared wall and an agreed working window. *(Link: Mural Artist, GATED.)*
- COMMISSIONED CANVAS. Painted off site and delivered finished. Suits boardrooms and walls that cannot be painted. Worth knowing: it can move with you if the office does. *(Link: Custom Canvas.)*
- ART SERIES. Several works commissioned as one. Suits corridors, floors and meeting rooms. Worth knowing: a series is planned as a whole, even when it is delivered in phases. *(Link: Coordinated Artworks.)*
- DIMENSIONAL WORK. A piece with depth or structure. Suits atria, voids and double-height spaces. Worth knowing: weight, fixings and access are part of the brief. *(Link: Art Installations.)*

**Composition:** a four-row comparison in type, three narrow columns per row, one small image per row at the left. The images are varied originals so the page shows range without a gallery.

### 6 · Working around an occupied office

**Purpose:** show that delivery in a live workplace is understood.

**Headline:** WORKING AROUND AN OFFICE THAT IS STILL IN USE.

**Copy**

Painting in an occupied building is a scheduling and protection problem as much as an artistic one. We plan zone by zone: what is protected, what is closed and when, which materials suit the space and how each area is cleaned down.

Evening and weekend working can be arranged where the building and your facilities team allow it. We plan to keep disruption low. We do not promise none.

- WORKING WINDOWS
- PROTECTION
- MATERIAL CHOICE, NOISE AND ODOUR
- PHASING BY ZONE

**Composition:** left, a three-image sequence (protected area, work in progress, cleaned-down result) at one height; right, a compact phasing diagram showing zones A, B, C with time blocks. The five labels sit as a ruled list under the paragraph.

**Media:** ESSENTIAL: a real preparation and protection sequence. TO SOURCE (priority). ILLUSTRATIVE phasing diagram, labelled. No night or weekend promise appears in any caption.

### 10 · Budget and fee factors

**Purpose:** explain what a workplace art budget is made of.

**Headline:** WHAT A WORKPLACE ART BUDGET IS MADE OF.

**Copy**

Cost follows the number of works and areas, whether each is an original or part of a series, the artist, the medium, the dimensions, framing and fixings, access and phasing.

A single reception piece has one decision maker, one working window and one installation. A multi-floor programme shares one direction but needs phased production and a schedule that holds across teams.

We scope each from the spaces you send, not from a headline figure.

### 11 · Questions, guides and the workplace brief

**Purpose:** clear the facilities and brand buyer's objections and close.

**Headline (FAQ):** THE OFFICE QUESTIONS.

**FAQ**

1. **Can you work from our brand guidelines?** Yes. Guidelines are a starting input, not a template. We read them for colour, tone and values, then develop an artistic direction for the architecture and audience.
2. **How do you choose an artist for an office?** By reading the architecture, the audience and the brand, then selecting the artist whose language suits them. A reception wall and a boardroom may call for different hands.
3. **Is this for one piece or a programme?** Either. One reception wall is a complete project. A programme across floors is planned as a whole and delivered in phases.
4. **Can you install while staff are in the building?** Often, with planning. We work zone by zone, protect surfaces, choose materials for the space and tell you what to expect before work starts.
5. **Can you work at night or at weekends?** Where the building and your facilities team allow it. It is agreed during scoping and is not assumed.
6. **How do you work with our designer and contractor?** We work beside them. They keep the interior; we deliver the artwork and the production around it, and agree the interfaces in writing.
7. **What if we move or rebrand?** Canvases and panels can move with you. Murals can be repainted or adapted, and we tell you early which parts of a commission are portable.
8. **Do we see concepts before we commit?** You appoint us first, and the concepts are developed inside the scope you agreed. Review rounds are set out in that scope and are not unlimited. Before that we go through the brief, the spaces and feasibility with you.

**Headline (guides):** ART PROGRAMMES, EXPLAINED.

**Six guides** (see section E).

**Final brief, headline:** WHERE SHOULD THE ART GO?

**Final brief, copy:** Where it is, what is being built or refreshed, which spaces you have in mind and roughly when. Photos and plans help but are not required. If you are not sure where art belongs, say that too.

**Composition:** as the shared closing pattern (dark surface, two ruled direct routes, form as the alternative). The FAQ is a single accordion with every answer in the HTML.
