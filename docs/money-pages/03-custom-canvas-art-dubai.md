## B. Page architecture and copy

### 1 · Original-work hero

**Purpose:** classify the purchase in one screen: a painted original, made for a space, not an upload-to-print service.

**Headline (H1):** CUSTOM CANVAS ART DUBAI.

**Copy**

- Statement: PAINTED BY HAND, FOR ONE WALL.
- Support: Original canvas paintings commissioned for commercial spaces and private interiors in Dubai and the UAE. A statement work, an oversized canvas, a diptych or a series, by the artist whose language suits the room.
- Qualifier in monospace under the support: ORIGINAL PAINT ON CANVAS. NOT A PRINT. NOT A REPRODUCTION.

**Composition:** paper surface. A tall finished canvas fills the right half edge to edge from top to base, shown at its true proportions, with the wall label beside it. H1, statement and support in the left half, CTA below. The logo is top left. Mobile: the canvas first, full width and uncropped, then the type.

### 2 · Material and making

**Purpose:** show authorship, texture and the physical presence of paint.

**Headline:** YOU CAN SEE THE HAND.

**Copy**

A painted canvas carries what a print cannot: texture, depth, the direction of the brush and the weight of a real object on a wall.

Every commission is painted by an artist, on a canvas or panel built for its size.

Caption on the detail: DETAIL / BRUSHWORK AT ACTUAL SCALE

**Composition:** one oversized detail crop that bleeds off the left edge and the top of the section, with the two sentences set small in the right margin. Beneath it, a three-frame sequence from pencil to finished work at one height.

**Media**

- ESSENTIAL: one macro detail of real paint surface, photographed, not a print close-up. EXISTS in part (detail crop of `PORSCHE_PORTRAIT` or `FERRARI_REALISM`; confirm it shows paint, not printed texture).
- ESSENTIAL: artist-hand sequence. EXISTS: `CANVAS_PROCESS` (pencil lines to finished canvas).

### 3 · Commission types

**Purpose:** let the buyer name what they need.

**Headline:** ONE WORK OR A FAMILY OF WORKS.

**Copy** (ruled rows)

- STATEMENT WORK. One canvas that carries the room.
- OVERSIZED CANVAS. Made at the scale of the wall. A very large work may be built in sections and assembled on site.
- DIPTYCH OR TRIPTYCH. Two or three panels that read as one image across a wall or a corner.
- COORDINATED SERIES. Related originals for adjacent rooms or a long route.

Closing line in monospace: Dimensions are shown only where we know them.

**Composition:** four rows at full width. Each row has its name in display type and one finished or in-progress image at its right, at its own aspect ratio. Rows are different heights, so the section has rhythm instead of a grid.

**Media:** ESSENTIAL: one image per type, finished or making. EXISTS for statement work and canvas in progress; TO SOURCE for diptych, triptych and series. If a type cannot be shown, drop its image but keep its text row.

### 4 · Project-led artist matching

**Purpose:** show that the artist is chosen for the brief, with no fixed catalogue of styles.

**Headline:** THE ARTIST FOLLOWS THE ROOM.

**Copy**

We have no house style, and we do not ask you to choose from one. We look at the room, the brief and the people who will live with the work, then select the artist whose language fits. It might be photoreal, graphic or more experimental.

Four things guide the choice: the scale of the work, the palette and light of the room, how close people will stand, and what the piece is there to do.

We would rather tell you early that a brief is a poor fit than deliver it badly.

**Composition:** three works of visibly different character shown side by side at three different heights, each with its label (title, artist, medium). The four selection criteria sit as a ruled list in monospace below.

**Media:** ESSENTIAL: two or more visibly different original approaches with authorised artist credits. EXISTS in part: `CANVAS_PROCESS` (painterly) and `PORSCHE_PORTRAIT` (photoreal). TO SOURCE: further canvases by other artists. **Artist credit is required before any work is published here.**

### 5 · Artwork in the room

**Purpose:** show that scale, light and viewing distance drive the commission.

**Headline:** MEASURE THE ROOM BEFORE YOU CHOOSE THE SUBJECT.

**Copy**

Scale is the first decision. We work from the wall's proportions, the furniture around it, the light across the day, the viewing distance and the way people arrive in the room.

Send a photograph and rough measurements and we will suggest a range of sizes before an artist is appointed. A visualisation for your project follows appointment, inside the agreed scope.

**Composition:** left, an elevation drawing of a wall with a standing figure outline and three canvas rectangles at different sizes, drawn in thin line and labelled "Illustrative". Right, a real photograph of material swatches or a canvas edge. The text sits under both.

**Media:** ESSENTIAL: ILLUSTRATIVE elevation graphic. OPTIONAL: real material swatches and a canvas-edge detail. TO SOURCE. No free project-specific mockup is offered or implied.

### 6 · An existing design or an open commission

**Purpose:** show the two entry paths and the rights position.

**Headline:** A DESIGN YOU HAVE, OR ONE WE DEVELOP.

**Copy**

- THE DESIGN EXISTS. You have an approved palette, scheme or artwork reference. We adapt it where the rights allow. We do not copy a living artist's signature work.
- THE COMMISSION IS OPEN. You describe the room and what the piece should do. After appointment, the artist develops the work for your review.

What helps: a photograph of the wall, its dimensions (or "unsure"), a palette or references, and your deadline.

**Composition:** two columns, a hairline between them, and the helpful-inputs line in monospace beneath. No image.

**Media:** none.

### 7 · Approval and production

**Purpose:** show the path and make revisions a scope term, not an open promise.

**Headline:** HOW A COMMISSION IS PAINTED.

**Copy**

1. SCOPE AND ARTIST. We define the commission and select the artist.
2. APPOINTMENT. You appoint us; development begins.
3. DEVELOPMENT AND REVIEW. The artwork is developed for your review. Review rounds are set in the scope.
4. PAINTING. Progress is shared at the stages agreed in the scope.
5. FINISH AND FINAL APPROVAL. The finished work is reviewed before delivery.

Line beneath: Revisions are a scope term, not an open promise: how many review rounds, at which stage, before painting begins.

**Composition:** a five-frame making progression across the full width (sketch, underpainting, development, detail, finished), each frame labelled with its step. Text beneath, not beside.

**Media:** ESSENTIAL: a making progression of one real piece. EXISTS: `CANVAS_PROCESS` (clip and poster). OPTIONAL: stills from a larger commission as they are documented.

### 8 · Framing, packing and installation

**Purpose:** say exactly what is included and what is by a specialist.

**Headline:** FINISH, FRAME, DELIVER.

**Copy**

What is included is stated in the proposal. Framing or edge finish, protective packing, transport and installation are separate lines of scope, supplied by the studio or by a specialist, never assumed.

Fixings, access and wall conditions are reviewed before the work is delivered, so the piece arrives ready to hang.

**Composition:** a row of three small, close photographs at one height (edge and corner, packing, work on the wall) with the two paragraphs beside them.

**Media:** ESSENTIAL: one edge or finish image. TO SOURCE. OPTIONAL: packing and handover photographs. TO SOURCE. Do not assume the studio supplies framing or installation in every case.

### 10 · Commission costs and timing

**Purpose:** give the serious commissioner the variables without publishing a rate.

**Headline:** WHAT DETERMINES COST AND TIME.

**Copy**

Cost follows the artist, the size, the medium and level of detail, the number of works, the creative scope, the finish, framing, transport and installation, and the deadline.

A painting cannot be hurried past its drying and finishing. Size, detail and the artist's method set the schedule, and your deadline sets what is feasible. We confirm both in the proposal.

**Composition:** two columns of ruled type, COST on the left, TIME on the right. Quiet.

**Media:** none.

### 11 · Questions, guides and the commission brief

**Purpose:** settle originals, approvals and logistics, then collect useful room context.

**Headline (FAQ):** ABOUT THE COMMISSION.

**FAQ**

1. **Is this an original painting or a print?** An original, painted by hand by an artist. Photo-to-canvas printing and ready-made décor are different products, and not what this page covers.
2. **How is the artist chosen?** By the brief. We select the artist whose language fits the room, the scale and the purpose, or work with an artist you already have in mind. There is no catalogue of house styles.
3. **Do you take both commercial and private commissions?** Yes: hotels, workplaces, retail interiors, residences and development projects, from a single piece to a small family of works.
4. **How large can a canvas be?** As large as the wall and the access allow. Very large works may be built in sections and assembled on site. We review the wall, the access and the handling before proposing a size.
5. **Can you match our palette or an existing design?** Yes. Send the palette, scheme or references. We adapt an approved direction where the rights allow, and we do not copy a living artist's signature work.
6. **Do we see a concept first, and how do revisions work?** The concept comes after appointment, inside the agreed scope. Review rounds and the stage at which each takes place are set in the scope. They are not unlimited.
7. **Are framing and installation included?** They are scoped separately and never assumed. The proposal states what the studio supplies and what a specialist supplies.
8. **How long does it take?** It depends on size, detail, the artist's method and drying and finishing time. The proposal gives a programme, and we say plainly if a deadline is not feasible.
9. **Who owns the work, and is it signed?** Ownership of the physical painting, any right to reproduce the image, the artist's credit and the signature are agreed in the commission. We do not assume them.

**Headline (guides):** READ BEFORE YOU BRIEF.

**Final brief, headline:** SEND US THE WALL.

**Final brief, copy:** A photograph of the space is the most useful thing you can send. Rough measurements help, "unsure" is fine, and so is an idea that is still taking shape.

**Composition:** shared closing pattern. The form accepts an image upload as an optional first-class field.
