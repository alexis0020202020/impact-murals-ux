### 2 · Hospitality art opportunities

**Purpose:** show where art goes in a hotel and the decision each place raises.

**Headline:** ALONG THE ROUTE.

**Copy** (a ruled list)

- ARRIVAL AND LOBBY. The first picture of the hotel. One anchor work, or two in conversation.
- RESTAURANTS AND BARS. Art that works in low light, and for a different crowd after dark.
- SUITES AND GUEST ROOMS. Quieter and more personal, with variation across room types.
- CORRIDORS AND LIFT LOBBIES. A rhythm of works that guides a guest without signage.
- SPA AND WELLNESS. Calm palettes and materials chosen for humidity and cleaning.
- POOL, TERRACES AND FEATURE AREAS. Work chosen for sun, wind and exposure.

Closing line: Commission one area now and add others later. The direction can be set for all of them.

**Composition:** the six lines sit on the same grid as the section drawing from the hero, which reappears here larger and annotated, each mark aligned to its line. All text is visible without interaction. Mobile: lines stacked, drawing reduced to a thin route line.

### 4 · Fit to the interior

**Purpose:** show that the art is developed from the design narrative and leaves the interior to the designer.

**Headline:** DEVELOPED WITH THE INTERIOR.

**Copy**

We start from the designer's narrative: palette, materials, lighting, proportions and how a guest moves through the building. Plans, elevations, moodboards and FF&E schedules are enough to begin.

The art adds a layer of authorship to the scheme and leaves the interior in the designer's hands.

**Composition:** editorial two columns. Left, a plan excerpt with one location marked, labelled "Illustrative plan"; right, a row of material samples beside the text. Nothing here implies a real hotel.

### 5 · Package and responsibility

**Purpose:** define the commissioned scope and the interfaces. This is the page's trust module.

**Headline:** WHAT WE TAKE ON, AND WHERE WE STOP.

**Copy**

- OPERATOR OR OWNER. The brief, the approvals and the opening date.
- INTERIOR DESIGNER OR ARCHITECT. The design narrative and the scheme.
- ART OR FF&E CONSULTANT, IF APPOINTED. The wider collection and its procurement.
- CONTRACTOR. Walls, ceilings, access and fixed installation, as scoped.
- IMPACT MURALS. Art direction or adaptation, artist selection, production and delivery of the commissioned art.

We are not your FF&E consultant, and we do not procure a whole property's art. We make the commissioned pieces and work comfortably beside whoever holds the rest.

What a commission can include, each as scoped: ARTIST AND DIRECTION, SAMPLES, PRODUCTION, PACKING AND DELIVERY, INSTALLATION COORDINATION, HANDOVER AND CARE NOTES.

**Composition:** a five-row ledger on the left, the checklist of inclusions on the right as a quiet monospace column. Hairline rules only. No icons.

**Media:** none. (This module is type.)

### 6 · New opening or operating hotel

**Purpose:** show the two programme tracks.

**Headline:** A NEW OPENING, OR A HOTEL THAT IS ALREADY OPEN.

**Copy**

- NEW OPENING. Art is timed to the handover. Approvals, samples and production are scheduled backwards from the opening date, and the work arrives when the site is ready for it. Where a model room or sample review is part of the programme, we build it in.
- OPERATING HOTEL. Work is phased by wing or floor around guest routes. Working windows, noise and odour are planned with the hotel, and rooms are released in sequence. We plan around the guests who are staying. We do not promise no disturbance.

**Composition:** two parallel horizontal tracks, one above the other, each with five milestone markers; the text for each track beneath its line. Vertical and stacked on mobile.

**Media:** OPTIONAL: real site or process imagery for each track. TO SOURCE. ILLUSTRATIVE tracks, labelled.

### 7 · Finish, use and care

**Purpose:** answer durability honestly, without a blanket guarantee.

**Headline:** BUILT FOR A BUILDING THAT IS USED.

**Copy**

Corridors have trolleys. Restaurants have cleaning crews. Lobbies have sun. The finish and the fixing are chosen for the medium and the place, and we write down how each work should be cleaned and handled.

We do not prescribe one coating for every material, and we do not offer a blanket guarantee.

**Composition:** three macro images in a row at one height, each with a monospace use note beneath (for example: HIGH-TRAFFIC CORRIDOR / FINISH CHOSEN FOR THE MEDIUM). Use notes appear only when the sample's real finish is known.

**Media:** ESSENTIAL: authentic detail and sample images with use notes. TO SOURCE. EXISTS in part: macro details from existing work (no finish data yet).

### 9 · Hospitality commission process

**Purpose:** show the path with its programme dependencies.

**Headline:** FROM BRIEF TO THE GUEST'S FIRST LOOK.

**Copy**

1. BRIEF AND SCOPE. The spaces, the opening or refurbishment date, the stage of design.
2. APPOINTMENT. Scope and responsibilities agreed in writing.
3. CONCEPT OR ADAPTATION. We develop the direction, or adapt the approved one.
4. SAMPLE AND APPROVAL, WHERE NEEDED. A sample or model-room piece is approved before production.
5. PRODUCTION SCHEDULE. Set against site readiness and the other trades.
6. SITE AND INSTALLATION. Coordinated with the contractor and the operator.
7. HANDOVER. Care notes and a record of the works.

**Composition:** a horizontal sequence of seven nodes on a single line, two of them (sample approval and site readiness) drawn as dependency markers. Vertical on mobile.

**Media:** none.

### 10 · Budget scope

**Purpose:** explain scope drivers and separate a statement piece from a programme.

**Headline:** A STATEMENT PIECE OR A COORDINATED COMMISSION.

**Copy**

Cost follows whether the work is a single original or a series, the quantities and sizes, the artist, the creative scope, samples, materials, framing and fixings, access and phasing, and care requirements.

We do not give a per-room price. A lobby commission and a programme across every floor are different projects with different decision structures. Send the spaces and the stage you are at, and we will scope what is realistic.

**Composition:** two columns, A STATEMENT PIECE and A COORDINATED COMMISSION, with three contrasts each (who decides, how many samples, how it is installed). Type only.

### 11 · Questions, guides and the project brief

**Purpose:** settle the procurement team's questions and ask for the right information.

**Headline (FAQ):** PROCUREMENT QUESTIONS.

**FAQ**

1. **Can you work with our art or FF&E consultant?** Yes. They hold the wider collection; we deliver the commissioned pieces and agree the interfaces in writing.
2. **Do you only take full packages?** No. One lobby commission is a complete project. A defined package across several areas is another.
3. **Do we need an approved design first?** No. We can develop the direction with you, or adapt the one your designer has approved. Concepts are developed after appointment, inside the agreed scope.
4. **Can you work in a hotel that is open?** Yes, phased by wing or floor with working windows, noise and odour planned with the hotel. We do not promise that guests will notice nothing.
5. **Do you make samples or model-room pieces?** Where the programme calls for it, a sample or model-room piece is scoped and approved before production.
6. **How do the works hold up to cleaning and traffic?** The finish is chosen for the medium and the location, and care notes are written for each work. We do not offer one blanket guarantee.
7. **Who installs the work?** The scope states who installs: the studio, a specialist or your contractor, depending on the work and the site.
8. **What happens at refurbishment?** A record of the works and care notes are handed over, so a future refurbishment starts from facts. Repeat or replacement production is agreed case by case.
9. **Are the pieces originals, editions or reproductions, and where do you work?** Originals. We take projects in Dubai and across the UAE, case by case.

**Headline (guides):** FOR THE PROCUREMENT FILE.

**Five guides** (see section E).

**Final brief, headline:** BRING THE BRIEF, OR THE OPENING DATE.

**Final brief, copy:** Property, location, opening or refurbishment, the spaces and roughly when. Plans and room types help but are not needed to begin. If the art consultant or designer is already appointed, tell us who.

**Composition:** shared closing pattern; the form shows the stakeholder role as the first question.

**CTA:** DISCUSS A HOTEL ART PROJECT.
