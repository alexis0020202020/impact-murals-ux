### 2 · What guests receive

**Purpose:** show the result and the experience, with the supplied product and technique stated.

**Headline:** ONE OBJECT BECOMES THEIRS.

**Copy**

A guest chooses from a short menu. The artist makes the object theirs while they watch. They leave with a piece nobody else has, and the memory of how it was made. The design can be a painted motif, a drawn detail or their name in lettering.

Frame captions: SUPPLIED BY THE BRAND / IN THE ARTIST'S HAND / FINISHED

**Composition:** a three-frame horizontal sequence of one object, at one height, captions in monospace. The same object appears in all three frames.

**Media:** ESSENTIAL: a genuine before, during and after sequence of one object. TO SOURCE. A clearly labelled studio demonstration is acceptable if it is captioned as one.

### 5 · Guest journey

**Purpose:** show which part of the experience a guest waits for.

**Headline:** FROM CHOICE TO COLLECTION.

**Copy** (five steps)

1. CHOOSE. The guest picks from a short menu.
2. MAKE. The artist works while they watch.
3. SET. The piece dries or finishes.
4. PACK. It is wrapped under your brand.
5. COLLECT. The guest takes it, or it is delivered.

The wait guests notice is often drying and collection, not painting, so we plan them as a flow of their own.

**Composition:** a horizontal five-frame flow with steps 3 and 4 drawn as the waiting stages (a hatched band behind them). Text sits beneath each frame, not inside it.

**Media:** ESSENTIAL: five frames. TO SOURCE. ILLUSTRATIVE frames, labelled, are acceptable until real ones exist.

### 6 · Capacity and station design

**Purpose:** meet the throughput objection directly and show the system.

**Headline:** HOW MANY CAN WE FINISH?

**Copy**

Throughput is the honest question, so we answer it first. We work it out from your brief, not from a number quoted in the abstract: how long an approved design really takes on your product, how many artists and stations the space allows, how many guests arrive at once, and the time lost to breaks, handling and rework.

A short, tested menu moves faster than unlimited choice. Unlimited choice suits a small VIP room.

We do not promise a number until the design has been tested on your product.

**Composition:** left, the station diagram: a queue, a menu board, one or more artist stations, a drying area, packing and collection, drawn in thin line, labelled "Illustrative station layout". Right, the three paragraphs. A sample menu card (three options, labelled "Illustrative menu") sits under the diagram.

**Media:** ESSENTIAL: ILLUSTRATIVE station diagram and menu card. OPTIONAL: a real station photograph. TO SOURCE.

### 7 · Product responsibility and samples

**Purpose:** divide responsibilities visibly and explain testing.

**Headline:** WHO SUPPLIES WHAT, AND HOW WE TEST.

**Copy**

- YOUR SIDE. The products, the stock including spares for damage or rework, and any manufacturer guidance on the material.
- OUR SIDE. The artist or artists, the materials, test pieces, the handling plan and the finish.
- TOGETHER. Sample approval, menu approval, and who is responsible if a piece is damaged.

We test on your actual product before any menu is promised. Samples are developed after appointment, or in an agreed paid test, so you are never asked to approve speculative work. We do not promise water or wear resistance beyond what the tested finish supports.

**Composition:** three columns of type above a row of material test images at one height, each with a monospace note naming the material and the finish.

### 10 · Cost drivers and alternatives

**Purpose:** explain what shapes the fee and when live is the wrong choice.

**Headline:** THE FEE, AND WHEN NOT TO GO LIVE.

**Copy**

The fee follows the technique, the quantity, the complexity of the menu, the number of artists and stations, the hours, the samples, the stock and packaging, the venue and the finish.

- LIVE, when the interaction is the experience.
- PRE-PRODUCED, when the object carries more weight than the process: a hero piece, a gift, a small edition.

Both can sit in one project. For pre-produced hero pieces and editions, see Artist Collaborations and Commissioned Brand Artwork.

**Composition:** two columns, type only, with the closing sentence as a quiet pull note.

**Media:** none.

### 11 · Questions, guides and the activation brief

**Purpose:** resolve material, capacity and guest-experience objections, then close.

**Headline (FAQ):** THE PRACTICAL QUESTIONS.

**FAQ**

1. **Which products and materials can be customised?** Footwear, bags and small leather goods, accessories, packaging, bottles and gifts are common. Whether a technique suits a material is confirmed with a sample, never assumed.
2. **Who supplies the products?** Usually the brand, including spares. Stock, packaging and responsibility for damage are agreed in the proposal.
3. **Which techniques do you offer?** Hand painting, illustration, and lettering or calligraphy. Printing, laser engraving and hot foil are different services.
4. **How is it tested and approved?** We test the technique on your actual product, then approve a short menu with you. Both steps follow appointment, or an agreed paid test.
5. **How many pieces can be finished in the event window?** It depends on the design, the product, the number of artists and stations and how guests arrive. An illustrative figure is not a tested one. We give a number only after the design has been tested on your product.
6. **How many artists and stations will we need?** That is set from the tested time per piece and the expected arrival pattern, with allowances for breaks and rework. More stations shorten queues but need space and stock.
7. **How are drying and collection handled?** As their own flow: time or racks for setting, packing, and collection or delivery. Some pieces can go home at once. Others are collected later or delivered. The plan says which.
8. **What if a product is damaged or the manufacturer restricts the material?** Spares are planned for rework, responsibilities are agreed in the proposal, and any manufacturer or safety restriction is confirmed before production.

**Headline (guides):** THE FLOW, IN DETAIL.

**Six guides** (see section E).

**Final brief, headline:** SHOW US THE PRODUCT.

**Final brief, copy:** The product, how many guests, the hours and the venue. A photograph of the product helps most. If you are not sure which technique suits it, ask us to recommend one.

**Composition:** shared closing pattern; the form's first optional field is a product photograph.
