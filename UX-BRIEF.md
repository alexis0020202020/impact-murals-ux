You are taking over the next UX/UI implementation pass on Impact Murals.

Work from the corrected V2-80 sources in this project directory. This is an implementation task: inspect, design, experiment, implement, render, use, critique, correct and verify. Do not deliver another audit instead of making the improvements.

The objective is a coherent, distinctive, commercially clear creative-studio website. You have substantial freedom over the presentation, but not over the protected technical systems.

## 2. What the website needs to communicate

Impact Murals is an artist-led art direction and production studio working across brands, places and public art in Dubai and the UAE.

Murals are a core expertise, not the limit of the studio.

A visitor should quickly understand:

- what the studio does;
- how art can contribute to their particular project;
- that Impact Murals can conceive AND realise the work;
- that it can select the right artist or visual language;
- that it can join an existing team or execute an approved direction;
- how to start a conversation without having a finished brief.

The site must not feel like a generic mural supplier, a full-service corporate agency, a SaaS landing page, a procurement manual, an opaque art portfolio or a generic luxury template.

It needs creative authority and commercial clarity at the same time.

Do not turn the site into an endless explanation of the company. Do not make it so abstract that clients have to interpret the offer.

## 3. Preserve three clear, flexible offers

### Art for Brands

Art as a strategic part of communication and experience: launches, events, activations, live painting, artist collaborations, branded murals, packaging and other communication applications.

Show how an artwork or activation can become something a brand communicates around, including visual content where relevant to the agreed scope.

Do not reduce this offer to painting logos. Do not automatically promise complete campaigns, media buying or a full video-production service.

### Art for Places

Art integrated into a physical space: commissioned canvases, bespoke artwork, murals and site-specific responses for interior designers, architects, hospitality, developers, workplaces and direct commissioners.

Make it clear that the studio understands an existing design intent and enriches it. The client is not forced into one fixed artistic style or a replacement interior-design service.

### Public Art & Large-Scale Murals

Artistic work tied to a real site and its execution constraints: facades, public realm, destinations, private sites, murals and technically demanding interventions.

Communicate the ability to develop or adapt a concept, select artists, consider surfaces and access, coordinate execution and realise an approved direction.

This is not only for government projects or enormous walls. Smaller projects needing specialist artistic execution should still feel welcome.

### Across all three

These are entry points, not rigid packages. Projects may overlap them.

Artist selection, creative direction and execution capability are shared strengths. Live painting and workshops are relevant formats, not competing top-level offers.

Keep enough concrete examples for people to recognise their need. “Less repetition” must not become “less useful information”.

Do not create compulsory qualification barriers around exact dimensions, budgets, finished concepts or project size.

## 4. Creative direction

The guiding principle is:

CLEAR IN PURPOSE.
LOOSE IN COMPOSITION.
PRECISE IN EXECUTION.

Use an underlying grid and deliberate hierarchy, but do not make the site feel trapped in boxes.

Explore whitespace, scale, asymmetry, image ratios, controlled overlaps, a few unexpected placements, typographic rhythm, quiet moments and selected energetic accents.

The visitor's mental model should remain simple even when the visual composition is expressive.

Keep what already works. You may rethink a weak composition completely, but not because every new pass needs a new concept.

Translate a useful principle into the Impact Murals identity. Do not copy another studio's layout, artwork, assets or visual identity. Do not present a reference as inspected if you could not open it.

The benchmark is the quality of hierarchy, rhythm, interaction and execution, not imitation.

### Logo and motion

Keep the Impact Murals logo and its fragmented/deforming animation. Do not replace it with a static wordmark to simplify the task.

Its fragmentation may inform a few masks, image edges, spatial transitions or secondary graphic elements. Avoid repeated floating decoration, confetti or unrelated effects.

The logo must coexist with a legible proposition and accessible CTA. Do not make visitors wait for the animation before they can understand or act.

No forced intro, scroll hijacking, essential hover-only information or moving-away buttons. Navigation and CTAs remain stable.

Use the existing animation system. Respect reduced motion. Design a calmer mobile version rather than shrinking desktop motion.

### Colour and typography

The current version is already predominantly light. Refine its balance rather than blindly converting it again.

Use a breathable canvas, purposeful dark moments and colour where it strengthens the identity. Avoid the formula “beige + black + giant slogans”. Do not compensate for provisional imagery by inventing an unrelated decorative world.

Use a refined contemporary grotesk direction, not condensed agency typography. Keep readable body text and useful intermediate levels, not only enormous headings and tiny descriptions.

Not every section needs a slogan. Some moments can be visual, concise or quiet.

Avoid em dashes in public-facing copy.

## 5. Page roles and visitor journeys

### Home

Orient, create interest and establish relevance. Make the three offers central and discoverable without a long preliminary manifesto.

The visitor should understand the studio, find a relevant project context, see how it can contribute and access a conversation.

The three offers need consistent recognition and interaction, not identical compositions. Avoid generic service cards, but do not remove useful labels to achieve a more artistic appearance.

Do not cram every offer and every proof into the first screen. Make the first screen useful, then guide the visitor naturally.

Integrate visual evidence into the offers where useful. Do not create a huge unrelated portfolio sequence that pushes the commercial proposition away.

Merge or remove redundant statements. Preserve useful reassurance.

### What We Do

Help visitors decide which situation is closest to their project. This is a routing page, not a duplicate homepage or an exhaustive capabilities catalogue.

Allow uncertainty and overlap. Visitors should not need to understand your internal classification before contacting you.

### Offer pages

Treat each as a possible first landing page from outreach or search.

Without first reading Home, someone should understand the offer, recognise relevant formats, see what the studio contributes, understand its flexible role and find the next action.

Avoid mandatory global blocks repeated everywhere: “In All Three”, long audience lists, the same five-step process and the same large generic closing statement.

Give each offer specific substance. Keep shared navigation and interaction conventions coherent. Concise does not mean vague.

### Work

Visual credibility is welcome; traditional agency case studies are not required.

Do not impose Challenge / Strategy / Solution / Results. Use imagery and accurate concise context, role, location or production detail when available.

Do not invent clients, projects, testimonials, collaborators or results. Do not fabricate examples to replace missing images.

Keep provisional assets usable for design work without portraying them as completed commissions. Preserve existing placeholder guards. No filters without a genuine content need.

Work must support the offer-led site, not replace its organising principle.

### Studio

Establish artistic thinking, human credibility, appropriate artist selection and real execution knowledge.

Do not turn this into a second process page. Do not invent permanent staff or make a curated network sound like a large corporate team.

### Enquiry

Make starting a conversation welcoming. An early idea or a defined execution brief should both fit.

Preserve all existing functional behaviour. The supplied source is in preview mode; do not silently turn it into live sending or imply that delivery is verified.

Keep the unsure path, optional project details, back navigation, validations and answer retention. Do not send real requests while testing.

### Articles and guides

Keep automatic Markdown-based presentation. No manual layout per article, no duplicated article content in templates and no new editorial system.

Check long headings, paragraphs, lists, tables, images, captions, contents navigation, breadcrumbs, related content and offer/enquiry links on representative development content.

These pages should belong to the same studio visually, while prioritising comfortable reading.

### Global navigation

Keep destinations and actions understandable on desktop, touch and keyboard.

Menus must be discoverable, dismissible and usable without hover. Preserve meaningful focus behaviour, current-location cues where appropriate, and an accessible contact route.

Do not remove useful navigation merely to resemble a more experimental reference.

## 6. Mobile, accessibility and responsive composition

Mobile is its own composition, not a long vertical stack of the desktop grid.

Make intentional decisions about order, spacing, image scale, overlap removal and motion. Preserve business-critical information and access to actions.

Do not make long pages look shorter by shrinking essential text.

Use representative small/large mobile, tablet, short laptop and wide desktop viewports. Useful checks include 360x800, 390x844, 768x1024, 1280x720 and 1440x900; adapt to actual layout breakpoints, rather than treating these as the only valid screens.

Check at least:

- first-screen understanding and CTA access;
- long text and natural wrapping;
- horizontal overflow and cut-off content;
- touch targets and spacing;
- keyboard access, visible focus and menu dismissal;
- readable text and colour contrast;
- interactions during and after animations;
- reduced motion;
- image sizing and layout stability;
- runtime errors and obvious motion/performance problems.

Do not claim a formal accessibility certification or a performance score without the corresponding measurement.

## 8. Work autonomously and iterate at least 15 times

Use available UX/UI, frontend and browser skills when appropriate. Check their availability first. Impeccable, Design Audit, UI/UX Pro Max, Frontend Visual QA, Playwright or Chrome DevTools can help if actually installed; none is assumed to exist.

Use specialist guidance critically. The Impact Murals brief takes precedence over generic aesthetic recommendations, subject to technical and safety constraints.

Begin with a concise assessment of the actual baseline, then implement. Do not spend the whole session rewriting the brief or preparing a future to-do list.

You must perform AT LEAST 15 GENUINE REVIEW CYCLES.

A cycle is:

1. Inspect the current experience for a specific page, visitor task or quality dimension.
2. Identify a concrete weakness or confirm a specific strength.
3. Implement the correction where needed.
4. Inspect/test the resulting state again.
5. Record the evidence, result and remaining issue.

Use docs/UX-REVIEW-LOG.md. Its fifteen themes cover the minimum breadth; change the order or repeat themes when useful.

A cycle is not renaming a component, repeating the same screenshot or declaring “looks good”. Do not force arbitrary changes when the current solution is strong: record why it should remain and what you checked.

Repeated attempts at one weak composition are allowed. Compare alternatives when the choice materially affects comprehension or identity.

Continue beyond fifteen when significant, actionable, in-scope problems remain. Fifteen is a minimum, not a reason to stop.

Equally, do not create endless churn or claim objective perfection. Converge on a resolved result. Do not invent hours spent or fabricate completed reviews.

If a required visual check is blocked, mark it BLOCKED, not PASS. Continue safe work and report the limitation. Do not bypass permissions or claim completion of the fifteen cycles when they have not occurred.

Keep short progress updates, but do not ask for intermediate approval of ordinary reversible UX/UI decisions.

Do not return a first-pass result with a list of improvements you could safely have made yourself. Make those improvements and verify them.

If the session reaches an execution/context limit, update docs/UX-HANDOFF.md with exact progress and the next action. Do not mark an unfinished task complete.

## 9. Review from real prospect perspectives

Test concrete journeys, not only visual impressions:

- A brand manager arrives on Art for Brands for a launch or activation.
- An interior designer needs artwork that respects an existing scheme.
- An agency already has an approved direction and needs execution.
- A site owner needs exterior work and confidence in delivery.
- Someone wants a smaller mural, not an entire creative strategy.
- Someone has only an idea and cannot yet choose an offer.
- A reader arrives on an article and wants a relevant next step.
- A mobile visitor checks the studio after receiving a cold email.

For each, ask: can they recognise their need, understand the studio's contribution and find the next action without reconstructing the offer themselves?

These are expert scenario checks, not real user research. Do not label them user-tested unless actual participants were involved.

Then review as a demanding creative director and UX designer. Does the result have its own identity? Does its expressive composition make the experience stronger? Are weaker pages being hidden behind a polished homepage?
